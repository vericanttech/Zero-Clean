# What Zero-Clean Is About

## The Big Idea

**Zero-Clean** means **zero cleaning required afterward for training**. The app helps you build a photo collection of specific products in real stores in a way that is already organized and labeled. By the time you finish collecting, the dataset is ready to use for training—no extra cleanup step needed.

You are not just taking random photos. You collect according to clear rules: which store, which product, and in which situation (single product, on the shelf, or at checkout). You label where each product appears in each image and verify as you go. The goal is to end up with a **structured set of photos** that can go straight into training without any cleaning afterward.

---

## What You Do in the App

### 1. Set Up Your List

- **Stores**  
  You add the stores where you will collect (e.g. different supermarkets or shops). Each store is a separate "place" in your dataset.

- **Products (variants)**  
  You define exactly which products you care about. For each one you can specify things like category, brand, sub-brand, size, and packaging (e.g. can, bottle). The app turns that into a single product "variant" you can select when taking photos.

### 2. See Your Progress (Dashboard)

- You pick **one store** to focus on.
- You see **how many photos** you have in total and **how many product variants** you've captured.
- For each product variant you see **how far along you are** for that store in three situations:
  - **Single product** (one item, e.g. on a table)
  - **On the shelf** (product among others)
  - **At checkout** (e.g. on the belt or counter)

The app can show you which variants still need more photos ("under-collected") so you know what to shoot next.

### 3. Take Photos (Capture)

- You choose the **store**, **product variant**, and **situation** (single / shelf / checkout).
- You use the phone camera to take a photo. The app saves it and attaches the store, product, and situation you chose.
- There are simple targets (e.g. a certain number of photos per product per situation per store). The dashboard tells you when you're low so you can fill the gaps.

### 4. Review and Clean Up Your Photos

- You can browse all captured photos, including ones that are still "unprocessed" (not yet fully assigned to a store/product/situation).
- For any photo you can:
  - **Draw boxes** around each product visible in the image and label which product it is.
  - **Adjust** those boxes until they're accurate.
  - **Mark** when a photo or an annotation has been checked and is good to use ("verified").
- For photos that were taken without a product/situation selected (or that you want to reassign), you can **assign or change** the store, product, and situation. Once you confirm, the app moves the photo into the right place in your dataset and updates your progress.

So "review" is where you make sure every photo is in the right category and, where needed, has clear boxes around the products.

### 5. Keep Everything Organized

- Photos are stored in a clear structure: by store, by product, and by situation (single / shelf / checkout).
- You can add notes (e.g. lighting, angle, or if it's an unusual case). That helps keep the dataset "clean" and understandable later.
- The app tracks how many images you have per store, per product, and per situation, so you can see what's done and what's missing.

---

## Why This Exists

The point is to create a **reliable, labeled image dataset** of specific products in real retail environments—and to do it in a way that **requires no cleaning afterward**. By controlling **where** (which store), **what** (which product), and **how** (single, shelf, or checkout) you photograph, and by drawing boxes and verifying as you go, the collection is already clean and ready for training. You do the work upfront so there is no cleanup step later.

---

## In One Sentence

**Zero-Clean helps you collect and label photos of specific products in real stores in a structured way (by store, product, and situation) so that when you are done, the dataset is ready for training—with zero cleaning required afterward.**
